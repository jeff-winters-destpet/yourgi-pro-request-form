Mechanics this concept assumes (annotations, not UI)
  • Matched notifications: when a request lands, Pros who match it (service offered, coverage area, availability) get a push notification and come check the board. The board itself is the shared source of truth; the notification is just the doorbell.
  • Exclusive claim: pulling a card to Reviewing locks it to that Pro. Others see it grayed with the owner's name. (Server enforces the race: first claim wins.)
  • Claims expire: a claimed card auto-releases back to New Requests after 24h so requests don't rot in Reviewing. Shown on each claimed card.
  • Contact gating: the parent's phone/email stay masked until Accepted. Reviewing-stage communication happens through the platform (or concierge relay), which also protects the flat rate from off-platform deals.
  • Accepted = booked: accepting writes the appointment to the Pro's bookings list (right rail) at the quoted flat rate, and notifies the parent.
  • Payout vs. flat rate (open question): the parent pays the flat rate; the Pro sees their own payout. Under the Potemkin model Yourgi absorbs the difference, so these two numbers differ on purpose. The demo shows a placeholder payout - the real formula is a pricing/finance decision.
  • Address gating (open question): the detail view shows the neighborhood before accept and the full street address after. Dog walking arguably needs the address earlier; boarding does not. Decide per service.
  • Escalation: cards aging past a threshold with no claim get flagged to the concierge team (today's Teams channel keeps working as the fallback + audit log).
  • Master queue (Concierge role): Yourgi Concierge sees every request across every Pro, unfiltered and unmasked (matches what the Teams channel already shows concierge today). Concierge doesn't claim or accept; they oversee, and can force-release a stuck Reviewing card back to New.
  • Per-Pro filtering (Services & Pricing): each Pro's New Requests only shows what matches the services they've turned on, and disappears once they're at their weekly capacity or have paused new requests. Two Pros looking at the same instant see different queues by design. Settings are read-only here - a Pro changes them on the actual Services & Pricing page, not from the queue.
